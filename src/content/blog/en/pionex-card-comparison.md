Pionex Card suits people who already hold USDT and want to spend it abroad. RedotPay is worth investigating if you hold other coins. Crypto.com offers a different model: a prepaid card with rewards linked to its regional card tier and membership terms.

Look, there is no universal winner. Your country, the card actually offered to your account and your spending currency matter more than a headline cashback rate.

## Pionex Visa and Mastercard are not interchangeable

Our existing Pionex review quotes a 1% Visa purchase fee and Mastercard purchase fees up to 3.5%. It also describes up to 1% cashback and 5% APR on eligible balances. Availability, excluded purchases and the actual reward programme must be checked in your account.

Honestly, a free virtual card does not mean every purchase is free. Compare the card network, conversion costs and cashback you actually receive. Do not assume the advertised maximum reward applies to every transaction.

The existing review also quotes a Visa transaction limit of 10,000 USDT and a Mastercard limit of 20,000 USDT. For ATM use it quotes a 2% Visa fee plus any ATM operator charge. Treat these as dated published terms, not a promise about your account today.

## When an alternative is worth checking

**RedotPay:** our earlier comparison describes multi-asset funding and paid virtual and physical cards. It uses secondary reporting and a comparison written by Pionex. That is useful background, but Pionex has a commercial interest. Confirm issue costs, exchange charges, limits and country restrictions directly with RedotPay before choosing it.

**Crypto.com:** its official FAQ describes a prepaid card rather than a credit card. It says spending rewards are deposited in CRO. It also warns that top-up, ATM and exchange charges depend on jurisdiction and tier. A higher headline reward is not enough to justify a subscription or staking commitment on its own.

By the way, both the currency of the reward and the cost of qualifying for it matter. USDT spending, CRO rewards and card-balance interest are different things. None should be described as a guaranteed investment return.

## Country eligibility comes before cashback

Check two separate questions: can you complete account verification, and can the card pay merchants in your destination? These are not the same restriction list.

Start with our [Pionex eligibility guide](/en/blog/who-can-use-pionex-2026). Then consult the official country and card-usage sources below. A Visa or Mastercard logo does not guarantee approval at every merchant, or eligibility in every country.

## A practical decision checklist

1. Confirm your country of residence is supported by the provider.
2. Check which card network and programme your account is offered.
3. Compare issuance, purchase, conversion and ATM charges separately.
4. Read reward exclusions, monthly caps and any membership commitment.
5. Check funding assets and withdrawal options before moving money.
6. Try a small eligible payment before committing a larger balance.

For Pionex, use the [spending calculator](/en#calc) alongside your actual account terms. For an alternative, calculate its costs in the currency and regional programme you will use.

> Choose the card that fits your country and payment habits. Do not choose solely on the largest cashback number.
